Agnès Costa, the sister carrying Fragonard to its centenary

With Françoise and Anne, she runs the Grasse perfume house founded by her great-grandfather in 1926. Fragonard is marking its hundredth year with an exhibition at the Monnaie de Paris, opening on 13 November.

By Camille RostandPublished on Saturday, 7 November 2026Sources : Sortir à Paris, Groupe La Poste, Monaco Tribune, The Art Newspaper France, Centre Inffo, Objectif Gard, FragonardLire en français

In a few days, bottles from Grasse will move into the Quai de Conti. For its centenary, Fragonard is taking over the Monnaie de Paris with an exhibition combining perfumes, paintings and costumes, from 13 November to 10 January. It is the high point of an anniversary year the Grasse house has shaped in its own image: family-run, cultural and deeply Provençal.

Behind the brand are three sisters. Agnès, Françoise and Anne Costa run the house together, as La Poste noted in February when it issued a stamp for the centenary. Agnès and Françoise took over from their father, Jean-François Costa. They are the founder's great-granddaughters.

Yet nothing destined Agnès Costa for perfumery. She studied law, philosophy and English in Paris before joining the company in her early twenties. “I never grew up thinking I would one day take over the family business,” she told Monaco Tribune in 2021.

The story begins in 1926, when Eugène Fuchs bought a small perfumery in Grasse housed in an 18th-century former tannery and named it after the Grasse-born painter Jean-Honoré Fragonard. A few years later he handed the business to his son Georges and his son-in-law, François Costa. The historic factory still houses the company.

The family has made culture a pillar of the business. In 1997 Hélène Costa, the three sisters' mother, founded the Musée provençal du costume et du bijou in Grasse. The sisters have continued building collections, going as far as opening a fashion and costume museum in Arles. In March 2025 the art historian Philippe Costamagna became director of the perfumer's museums.

The model rests on an integrated network, from production to retail. Fragonard has three factories, six museums and twenty-five shops, according to Centre Inffo. The house keeps opening new outlets, such as in Nîmes in June 2025. It does not publish consolidated sales; its Grasse companies file separate accounts.

The centenary year has been punctuated by tributes. After the La Poste stamp, the Musée Jean-Honoré Fragonard in Grasse hosted the exhibition “Fragonard, l'enfant chéri de Grasse” (Fragonard, Grasse's beloved son) from June to October, devoted to the painter who gave the house its name.

A hundred years after Eugène Fuchs's purchase, Fragonard is still in the hands of the founding family. The three sisters nurture that independence by relying on what cannot be copied: a place, a history and a name.
